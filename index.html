Why this shape

Why home sessions with light dumbbells earn their place

Bone adapts to frequency of loading, not just intensity — one weekly gym session is a strong stimulus, but bone cells desensitize between stimuli and respond best to loading every 48–72h. Two short home sessions keep the signal alive between gym days. Your 10 kg dumbbells are enough because home work uses single-leg exercises (double the relative load), slow tempo, and impact drills (heel drops, stomps) — impact is one of the best-evidenced bone stimuli and needs no equipment at all.

Walking vs cycling for your bones

Cycling is non-weight-bearing — good for your heart, near-zero for bone (competitive cyclists often have low BMD). Brisk walking loads the hips and spine on every step. So: walking is your default cardio, and once it feels easy, add a loaded backpack (start 5 kg, build toward ~8–10% of body weight) — this is called rucking and directly loads both target sites. Keep cycling as an occasional swap, not the default.

Progression rules
  • Work at an effort of ~7–8/10: the last 2 reps of each set should feel hard but clean.
  • When you hit the top of the rep range on all sets with good form, move up one dumbbell step (or one machine plate) next time; at home, slow the tempo or add reps up to 15, then add backpack load.
  • Everything uses dumbbells, cables and machines — no barbell work. If months from now the heaviest dumbbells stop being challenging on the goblet squat, book one session with a trainer to learn the barbell versions.
  • Log your weights in the app — it shows your last weight on every exercise.
  • Every 6–8 weeks, take one lighter week (same exercises, ~60% weight).
Safety rules for osteopenia (read once, follow always)
  • Neutral spine under load. Chest proud, no rounding of the lower back on hinges/squats/rows. If form breaks, the set is over.
  • No loaded spinal flexion: skip weighted sit-ups, loaded toe-touches, heavy rounded-back movements. Core work here is anti-movement (planks, Pallof, dead bugs) — safer and better for the spine.
  • Impact stays moderate: heel drops, stomps, low hops — no depth jumps or max-height jumping.
  • Crohn's flare weeks: drop to easy walking + the warm-up mobility only. Resume at ~80% of previous weights.
  • Follow the report's medical advice separately: endocrinology consult, calcium + vitamin D. Training complements it, it doesn't replace it.
